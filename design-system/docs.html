01 — Overview

Design philosophy

THE SALON's visual system is minimal, elegant, timeless, and confident. It is editorial and architectural in construction — closer to a considered print publication or a gallery catalogue than a digital product. The governing standard is quiet luxury: restraint communicates the value of the room, not decoration.

The system draws on African design sensibility without resorting to cliché — no tribal-pattern borders, no earth-tone gradients standing in for "African," no motifs used as texture. Confidence is expressed through typography, proportion, and material color, not through symbols asked to carry cultural weight they were not designed to carry.

This is explicitly not an NGO aesthetic (no stock-photo warmth, no crowded infographics, no primary-color cheerfulness), not a startup aesthetic (no gradients, no rounded pill buttons, no glossy app-store polish), and not a corporate-conference aesthetic (no logo walls, no stage-backdrop clutter, no lanyard-branding energy). Reference points for calibration: Frieze, Kinfolk, The Gentlewoman, Vogue, and the restraint of an Apple keynote.

In practice this means large margins, generous white space, and beautiful typography carrying nearly the entire system. Nothing on the page is decorative. Every element — a rule, a label, a single glyph — earns its place by doing work: establishing hierarchy, marking a boundary, or standing in for a symbol the room already understands. When in doubt, remove the element rather than add one.

04 — Grid & Layout

Twelve columns, wide margins

Every desktop layout sits on a 12-column grid (--grid-columns: 12) inside margins of --margin-desktop (8vw) on each side. The margin is not empty space left over — it is a drawn line the content is never allowed to cross, the same discipline a print magazine keeps at its gutter.

Content rarely needs all twelve columns. A headline spanning six, a paragraph spanning five, a single pull-quote spanning four and centered — the unused columns are the point. Negative space is a design rule here, not decoration left behind: it is what makes a page feel considered rather than filled.

06 — Icon Usage

One icon. Exactly one.

The system has exactly one icon: the comb. No social icons, no decorative glyphs, no arrows-as-decoration, no set of line icons for "features" or "values." If a deliverable seems to need an icon, the correct response is nearly always typography or negative space instead — not a new glyph.

The comb (see next section) is the sole exception, and it is treated as art, not as an icon in a toolbar. Any request to introduce a second icon should be treated as a deviation from the system requiring deliberate, documented justification — not a default.

07 — The Heal by Hair Comb Symbol

The hero graphic

The comb is THE SALON's one visual signature — sourced from Heal by Hair. It functions as contemporary art, not as a logo: large, centered, and given room to be looked at rather than merely recognized.

Blue on white
White on blue

Clear space. Keep generous clear space around the comb: as a working minimum, no other element — text, rule, another symbol — should sit within one comb-height of its bounding box (illustrated by the dashed frame above). The comb should feel like it is resting in a room, not sharing a shelf.

Color. The comb always renders in a single flat color via currentColor — Bluemind Blue on white or ivory, or white/ivory on Bluemind Blue. Never a second color within the mark, never a gradient fill, never an outline-only or two-tone treatment.

Scale & role. Large and centered, treated as the singular art moment of a page — a cover, a section break, a closing screen. It is never used small, never repeated or tiled as a pattern, and never pressed into service as a bullet, a favicon-style mark next to running text, or generic decoration filling empty space.

Placeholder — not final art The comb geometry shown throughout this system (design-system/comb-placeholder.svg) is an abstract minimal placeholder — a rounded spine with nine tapered teeth — standing in for the real "Heal by Hair" comb artwork. That artwork is being redesigned by an artist and has not yet been delivered. Every deliverable using the comb today is using placeholder geometry and must be updated once the final SVG is provided; no deliverable should treat the current shape as finished, brand-approved art.
08 — Photography

No photography in 2026 launch materials

This is intentional, not a placeholder gap. Mystery is part of the launch — the room is defined by who is in it and what is said, not by a photo library. Photography returns once speakers and founding partners are confirmed and ready to be named.

No photography — 2026 launch

Speaker & Founding Partner Announcement Visual Approach

Once photography becomes appropriate (post speaker confirmation), maintain restraint: a single subject, tightly cropped portrait (shoulders-up), shot against a plain, unstyled backdrop (soft ivory or Bluemind Blue seamless paper — no environmental or office backgrounds), converted to a two-tone duotone treatment in Bluemind Blue and white (or Bluemind Blue and soft ivory) rather than full color. The comb glyph may appear as a small fixed eyebrow mark near, never overlapping, the face. Pair with the person's name and one-line role set in the display serif, and an "ANNOUNCING" eyebrow label in tracked-caps sans. No drop shadows, no saturated color blocks behind the portrait, no grids of multiple partner logos — one subject or one mark at a time, same generous margins as everything else in the system.

09 — Do & Don't

At a glance

Do

  • Single flat color fields — Bluemind Blue, white, and ivory used as solid surfaces
  • Hairline borders and rules (1px, currentColor) to mark boundaries
  • Generous negative space around every element, especially the comb
  • One focal element per screen or spread
  • Tracked-caps eyebrow labels for categorization (dates, acts, roles)
  • Display serif used sparingly, at scale, for genuine headline moments

Don't

  • Gradients of any kind, on any surface
  • Drop shadows, glossy or embossed effects
  • Photography in 2026 launch materials
  • Multiple accent colors active at once
  • Cluttered multi-logo partner walls
  • Decorative icons, arrows-as-decoration, or a second icon system
